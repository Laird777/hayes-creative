Luqsee: helping Google trust a resale marketplace

Luqsee · Preloved fashion marketplace · Local SEO, content & strategy · 2026

My role: Marketing Apprentice. I ran the SEO audit, wrote the strategy deck for the team, built the city landing-page template and wrote the blog posts.

Luqsee had products worth finding but gave Google very little to work with. I audited the site, turned the findings into a strategy the whole team could follow, and built the first pieces: a reusable city-page template for local search and a weekly run of blog posts.

Challenge

Hard to find in search

When someone searches "sell my clothes" or "consignment near me", Luqsee should show up. It rarely did. My audit found:

  • Thin product pages: about 40 words per product across a catalogue of 250+ items.
  • Generic page titles and no structured data.
  • Duplicate pages: two competing About pages, and Google was sending "About Us" searches to a sign-up form.
  • No local footprint: the homepage couldn't rank for every city Luqsee's partners serve.
  • Almost no backlinks from reputable sites.
Goal

Grow organic traffic and sign-ups

Give Google clear, consistent signals so Luqsee ranks for the searches its buyers and consignors make, starting with local "near me" searches.

Strategy

One idea a non-technical team could act on

SEO jargon loses a non-technical team fast, so I built the strategy around one simplified model: Google only recommends sites it feels confident about. Every duplicate page or thin description lowers that confidence. Every clean, consistent signal raises it.

I mapped six levers from where Luqsee was to where it needed to be. For example, product copy goes from about 40 words to 150–250 words of useful detail, duplicate pages become one clear page per job, and local presence goes from "just starting" to a page for every city served.

From my strategy deck
“We don't need five separate marketing miracles. We need to make Google trust us, and the rest follows.”

Why city pages? Luqsee's partner network is naturally local. A dedicated page per city proves local relevance with city names, neighbourhoods and local FAQs. The catch: pages that are too similar look like duplicates. So my template had to scale the SEO fixes while forcing real local detail into every page.

Execution

A template built to scale, and a weekly blog run

City landing pages. I built one repeatable page structure, then adapted it for each market with local copy, imagery, service areas, consignment categories and FAQs. Branding and structure stay the same; the local detail changes. A new market is an adaptation, not a rebuild.

Blog content. I wrote three posts for the Luqsee blog, published a week apart, each aimed at a question real consignors search for.

  • Seasonal Closet Swaps: What to Store, What to Sell, and How to Refresh Your Wardrobeluqsee.com · Aug 5, 2026
  • 10 Signs Your Clothes Are Worth Consigningluqsee.com · Aug 12, 2026
  • Ready to Consign? Here's How to Get Startedluqsee.com · Aug 19, 2026
Results

What shipped, and an honest note on measurement

  • An SEO audit and a strategy deck for the team, with a this-week / this-month / 90-day roadmap.
  • A reusable city landing-page template, adapted across multiple target markets.
  • Three published blog posts, bylined and live on luqsee.com.
  • A measurement plan, with the KPIs to track: retention by page, bounce rate, conversions and search rankings.
Measurement note

The pages were completed as my apprenticeship was ending, so post-launch data such as organic traffic, rankings and conversions wasn't available to me. I haven't presented any performance results as measured outcomes.
